Carnatic Music Notation Typesetter — Manual

← Back to the editor

The typesetter turns a simple text description of a song into a notation sheet laid out like a Carnatic music book: swaras and lyrics aligned against the tala, speed lines, stayi dots, gamaka signs and headings, in English, English with diacritics, Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam or Tamil.

This is version 3.0, a modern rebuild of Arun K's Carnatic Music Notation Typesetter v1.5 Beta. It reads the same input format, so notation files written for the old site work here unchanged. Small additions are marked new.

Using the editor

The window has the text you type on one side and the typeset page on the other. On a computer they sit side by side; press Layout in the toolbar to put the text under the page instead, and drag the divider to resize. On a phone, switch between Notation and Text at the top. Click any line of the page to jump to its S: line.

With the Live switch on (bottom of the text), the page redraws as you type; otherwise press Update there (or Ctrl/⌘+Enter). The badge next to it shows No issues, or how many problems were found — click it to see each one with its line number, and click a problem to jump to the line.

ButtonWhat it does

Open / SaveOpen or save the notation as a plain text file (⌘/Ctrl+S saves). Your work is also kept in this browser automatically, so a refresh does not lose it.

TAB completion: type the start of a directive name and press Tab (t → Tala: ). After Tala:, Language:, Layout:, Gati: and a few others, Tab completes the value too (Tala: k → KhandaCapu). On phones and tablets, a row of swara keys above the text saves switching keyboards.

The editor follows your device's light or dark appearance; the notation page always stays white, like paper.

Reading handwritten notation new

Read Photo reads a photo or scan of notation — handwritten or printed, with swaras and lyrics in English letters, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam or Devanagari — and writes it as input for this typesetter. You check and correct the result, then insert it.

Setting it up (once)

Reading images uses Claude, Anthropic's AI model, with your own API key:

  1. Create an account at platform.claude.com, add a little credit, and create an API key under API keys.
  2. In the typesetter press Read Photo, then Settings, and paste the key. It is stored only on this device (encrypted by the operating system in the desktop app) and sent only to Anthropic.

Each page costs a few US cents with the default model (Claude Sonnet 5.5); the more accurate Claude Opus 5.5 costs about twice as much. The photo is sent to Anthropic to be read; it is not stored by the typesetter.

Reading a page

  1. Press Read Photo and choose up to five images (on a phone you can take the photo directly). You can also paste an image or drop it on the window.
  2. Optionally tell it the script of the lyrics, the tala and raga if they are not written on the page, and anything unusual about the writer's style.
  3. Press Read notation. After 10–40 seconds you see the input it wrote, a preview of the typeset result, and notes about anything it was unsure of (also left as # comment lines).
  4. Fix anything that is wrong, then Insert at cursor or Replace all.

The result is checked with the same parser as your typing; if it finds mistakes, the reader is asked once to correct them automatically.

For the best results: photograph in daylight, flat and square-on, one page per photo, filling the frame. Crop away anything that isn't notation. Dots above and below swaras and the lines marking faster passages are the easiest things to lose, so check those first. Lyrics written in Indian scripts are converted to the roman transliteration this typesetter uses, so you can still switch the display language afterwards.

Input basics

Each line holds one directive: a name, a colon and a value. Names are case-insensitive and spaces around the colon don't matter. A few directives (SmartLyricMode, SongBreak, LineBreak, Pagebreak) have no value. Lines starting with # (or %) are comments, and blank lines are ignored.

Tala: Adi
S: s r g m | p d | n s'

Directives such as Tala, Layout, DefaultSpeed and SmartLyricMode belong in the header, before the first heading or swara line. Here is a complete short example (the "Quick Start" from the original manual):

Swaras — the S directive

S: notations
S: "label" notations

Notations are separated by spaces. Several S: lines in a row are joined into one continuous stream, which the typesetter breaks into lines at the end of each tala cycle (avartanam).

WriteMeaning
s r g m p d nA swara lasting one mātra in the current speed.
sa ri ga ma pa da niA dhīrgha (long) swara lasting two mātras. dha also works.
s'   p`Tara stayi (dot above) and mandra stayi (dot below). Repeat for two octaves: s''. Works with long swaras too: sa'.
,   ;Pause or continuation of one mātra and two mātras.
_   __Empty space of one or two mātras, with nothing shown (for indenting to the eḍuppu).
="Same as the previous line", lasting one whole akshara.
( … )Everything inside is twice as fast and gets a speed line. Nest them for faster speeds.
|   ||Optional, only for your own readability: the typesetter places tala markers itself (except in Tala: Manual).
"1)"A quoted label at the start is shown to the left of the line (sangati numbers, etc.).

Lyrics — the L directive

An L: line gives the lyrics for the S: line just above it. Add several L: lines for several caraṇams sung to the same tune.

By default you must give one entry for every swara and every pause in the swara line. Use _ for an empty entry; a . is shown as a dot (often used under a held note). You don't need to line the lyrics up with spaces, but the Align button will do it for you.

SmartLyricMode

Put SmartLyricMode in the header and you only need lyrics for actual swaras: pauses (, ; _ __ -) take an entry only if it is ., - or _. Swaras left without a lyric show a red ?.

Tala: Adi
SmartLyricMode
S: ; , ( p d n , ) pa m | ga , g | m pa , ||
L:       brO . va    bhA ra mA   ra ghu rA

Lyrics are typed in the Unified Transliteration Scheme and shown in the script chosen with Language.

Manual modes, LineBreak and SongBreak

Manual mode #1 (Tala: Adi,Manual): the typesetter still tracks the tala and places markers, but starts a new line only at LineBreak (which keeps counting the tala) or SongBreak (which starts a new tala cycle). Very long lines are wrapped at a phrase end or pause. Add ,InlineTalaMarkers to show the markers above the swaras instead (|| cycle start, | anga, * akshara), so that phrases are not split by bars.

Manual mode #2 (Tala: Manual): no tala at all. Markers appear exactly where you write | and ||. Useful for raga phrases or unusual layouts.

Layout and DefaultSpeed

LayoutDefault speedAlignment
Layout: krithi (default)second (2 swaras per akshara)rows aligned at each anga; swaras within an anga run together
Layout: varnamsecondrows aligned at every akshara, with a space between aksharas
Layout: gitamfirst (1 swara per akshara)aligned at every akshara

Layout: krithi,FullWidth stretches the rows to the full width of the page.

By default the bars (|) of rows in the same section line up, so a short anga gets extra space to match a longer one in the rows above or below. Layout: krithi,Compact turns this off: every row is set at its natural width. The options can be combined (Layout: krithi,Compact,FullWidth), and the Assist button offers them as checkboxes. (New in this version.)

DefaultSpeed: 0, 1 or 2 overrides the layout's speed: first speed (one swara per akshara), second (two in catuśra gati) or third (four). Use ( ) for faster passages; there is no way to go slower than the default.

Gati

Gati: tisra (or catusra, khanda, misra, sankirna) changes the gati from the next swara. With a default speed of 2, switching away from catuśra drops to speed 1 (3, 5, 7 or 9 swaras per akshara) and switching back restores it, as in practice.

DefaultSpeed: 2

SpeedMarks and PhraseEnds

SpeedMarks: above (default) or below puts speed lines over or under the swaras.

PhraseEnds: style or PhraseEnds: style,space controls phrase-end hyphens. Styles: Hide (default), Show (as a hyphen), Handle and HandleThick (a small corner after the swara). Space is in pixels (15) or inches (0.15in). Speed lines are broken at shown or spaced phrase ends.

Heading, Title, Pagebreak, Orientation

Heading: "Heading text",attribute,attribute,...

Attributes, in any order: a number (point size), bold, italic, underline, left/center/right, a language (english, diacritics, sanskrit, telugu, tamil, kannada, malayalam, optionally with attributes like tamil:noqual), or anything else, which is taken as a font name. Heading: "" adds a blank line.

Inside the text you can use *bold*, _italic_, ^superscript^, ~subscript~, [br] for a line break, --- for a horizontal rule, {c:red} at the start for a colour, {c:=} to underline, [img:https://…] for an image, and tables:

Heading: "*rAga:* AbhOgi[br]_tALa:_ Adi",center,13
Heading: "---"
Heading: "[|*P:*|nAdalOluDai brahmAnandamandavE manasA|\
|*A:*|svAdu phalaprada sapta svara rAga nicaya sahita|]",11

Title: "song name" sets the page title, which browsers can print in the page header. Pagebreak forces a new page when printing. Orientation: Landscape (or Portrait) sets the printed page orientation; you can add a margin such as Orientation: Landscape,0.5in.

Tamil

Because a lyric word is split into syllables (nin nu kO ri), the typesetter cannot tell where words begin. In lyrics, n is therefore always ன; write ^n or n2 for ந (ma na m ^nO gi). In headings, word starts are detected normally. Sounds Tamil does not distinguish get small qualifier numbers (க² க³ க⁴); turn them off with Language: Tamil:noqual. Tamil:granthasa uses ஶ for S.

Malayalam

Malayalam follows modern spelling: a consonant at the end of a word or lyric syllable takes its chillu form — n ൻ, N ൺ, r ർ, l ൽ, L ൾ — and a final m is written as anusvāra ം (avan അവൻ, manam മനം). Language: Malayalam:nochillu keeps the older form with a visible virama (അവന്). Swaras are printed സ രി ഗ മ പ ധ നി. (New in this version.)

LanguageFont

Every language has three built-in font styles, so the sheet looks the same on every device:

StyleFontsCharacter

The English font is also used for labels, numbers and headings. The Assist button on a LanguageFont line shows each style with a preview. (New in this version.)

Size and colour preferences

SwaraPrefs, LyricPrefs, HeadingPrefs and GamakaPrefs take a point size and/or a colour, e.g. LyricPrefs: 12,blue or SwaraPrefs: 12,rgb(64,100,125). The swara size is the largest size used; the sheet is scaled down automatically when a line would not fit the screen or page.

SwaraPrefs: 13,rgb(90,30,30)
LyricPrefs: 11,green
